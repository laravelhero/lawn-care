North Carolina sits in the tricky "transition zone" — the band of the country where it's too hot for cool-season grasses to be carefree, and too cold for warm-season grasses to stay green year-round. That means there's no single perfect grass. The right choice depends on your yard and your priorities.

Here's how the three most popular Triangle options stack up.

## Tall Fescue (cool-season)

Fescue is the most popular choice for Triangle lawns, and for good reason.

- **Stays green** through fall, winter, and spring
- **Tolerates shade** better than any warm-season option
- **Establishes fast** from seed

The trade-off: fescue struggles in peak summer heat and needs fall overseeding to stay thick, since it doesn't spread to fill in bare spots on its own. If you want a green lawn most of the year and have some shade, fescue is hard to beat.

## Bermuda (warm-season)

Bermuda is the workhorse of full-sun lawns.

- **Loves heat** and thrives in the worst of summer
- **Extremely durable** — perfect for kids, pets, and traffic
- **Self-repairs** by spreading aggressively

The catch: bermuda goes dormant and turns brown after the first frost, and it needs full sun. In shade, it thins out fast.

## Zoysia (warm-season)

Zoysia is the premium middle ground.

- **Dense, carpet-like** texture that feels great underfoot
- **Better shade tolerance** than bermuda
- **Chokes out weeds** once established

The downside is cost and patience — zoysia is slow to establish and more expensive up front. But many homeowners find the lush, low-maintenance result well worth it.

> No single grass wins on every metric. The best lawn starts with matching the grass to your sun, traffic, and tolerance for seasonal color changes.

## Quick recommendation

- **Shade or year-round green?** Choose fescue.
- **Full sun and heavy use?** Choose bermuda.
- **Want a premium, dense lawn and willing to invest?** Choose zoysia.
